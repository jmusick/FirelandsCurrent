## Erie County hazardous-waste collection is Saturday; registration required

Section: local · Community: Erie County · Byline: JD

Summary: The Oct. 10 collection at the county landfill is for district residents. Businesses and latex paint are excluded.

Erie County Solid Waste District residents must register before bringing household hazardous waste to the county landfill for a collection Saturday, Oct. 10.

The collection runs from 1-3 p.m. at the Erie County Landfill, 10102 Hoover Road in Milan. Businesses cannot participate, according to the [county's event notice](https://www.eriecounty.oh.gov/Events1.aspx).

Latex paint will not be accepted. The county directs residents to the registration form for the list of materials eligible for collection, so residents should check that list before loading their vehicles.

The [registration form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScat3egVnH96gSz4WMqBM3e3xvVzvh03zjBNyo-z9z3tJYD-Q/viewform) confirms the Oct. 10 date and says registration is required to attend. It asks for an email address on its opening page.

Residents with questions can call Environmental Services at 419-433-7303. The county also maintains a [household hazardous-waste page](https://www.eriecounty.oh.gov/HouseholdHazardousWastes.aspx) with disposal contacts for items such as motor oil, antifreeze and fluorescent bulbs. Those separate drop-off options may have restrictions or fees; call the listed provider before making a trip.

The acceptable-materials list is beyond the form's initial email step; no unverified list or price is asserted. Existing calendar event retained.

## Huron County to honor Extol, Graham and Plymouth coffee shop Wednesday

Section: business · Community: Huron County · Byline: JD

Summary: The Growth Partnership's free Oct. 7 awards event in Monroeville recognizes a Norwalk expansion, economic development work and a restored coffee shop.

Huron County Growth Partnership will honor a Norwalk manufacturer, a local economic development advocate and a Plymouth coffee shop Wednesday, Oct. 7, at its annual awards event in Monroeville.

Network After Work Annual Awards runs from 5-7 p.m. at the Monroeville Village Barn, 131 S. Main St. The [organizer's event listing](https://huroncountychamberofcommerce.growthzoneapp.com/member-events-calendar/Details/2026-network-after-work-annual-awards-1652440?sourceTypeId=Website) says attendance is free and provides a registration link.

Extol of Ohio will receive the Business Achievement Award for an expansion at its Norwalk facility. In its [Sept. 25 announcement](https://huroncountyohio.com/2026annualawards/), the partnership describes a $6 million project adding a 67,500-square-foot building and 35 jobs. The announcement does not say how many of those jobs have been filled.

In an [Aug. 27 announcement](https://extolohio.com/extol-of-ohio-named-2026-huron-county-growth-partnership-business-achievement-award-recipient/), Extol said the project nearly doubles its fabricating space and adds equipment for making industrial insulation. The company said the added space and equipment were already in use.

Donald M. Graham will receive the Pushing the Envelope Award. The partnership credits his work supporting employers, development projects and downtown revitalization in Willard.

The Entrepreneurial Spirit Award goes to The Coffee House of Plymouth, co-owned by Bruce Tanner and Katie Snook. The partnership said they acquired the property from the Huron County Land Bank in 2020, renovated the deteriorated house and opened the coffee shop in 2025.

The evening will include remarks from Team NEO CEO Matt Dolan and live music by The Locals, according to the partnership. Questions about the event can be directed to Kayla Cross at 419-217-1236.

## Huron County land-use survey seeks input on growth, farmland through Oct. 31

Section: government · Community: Huron County · Byline: JD

Summary: Residents can weigh in online or pick up a paper survey in Norwalk or Willard as the county updates its land-use plan.

Huron County residents have until Oct. 31 to weigh in on how the county should balance future development and farmland preservation as officials update its land-use plan.

The [Huron County Growth Partnership's survey announcement](https://huroncountyohio.com/landusesurvey/), posted Sept. 4, invites residents, property owners, businesses and other community stakeholders to participate.

The [online Community Land Use & Agriculture Survey](https://osu.az1.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_8tW91gIdQxeR1NI) was available Monday, Oct. 5. Its introduction asks residents about land use and where growth should occur and estimates the survey takes five minutes.

Paper copies are available at Norwalk Public Library, 46 W. Main St.; the Huron County Community Library's Willard location, 6 W. Emerald St.; and the Huron County Soil & Water District and Farm Service Agency location, 8 Fair Road in Norwalk, according to the announcement.

The partnership says feedback will help identify priorities for farmland, housing, infrastructure and other development needs. Responses will be considered alongside technical studies and other public input.

The [planning process announced in July](https://huroncountyohio.com/huron-county-launches-comprehensive-land-use-planning-process/) brings together county commissioners, the planning commission, the soil and water conservation district and the Growth Partnership, with Mannik & Smith Group as consultant. Ohio Department of Agriculture funding supports the work.

The July announcement also outlined three public engagement meetings. The survey announcement does not give meeting dates; project updates are available through the [partnership's news page](https://huroncountyohio.com/news/). Questions about the survey can be sent to outreach@huroncountyohio.com.
